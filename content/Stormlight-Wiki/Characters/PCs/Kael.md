# Kael

**Also known as:** —
**Status:** Deceased — died fighting [[Sebarial]] at his research outpost on the Shattered Plains, then passed into the Cognitive Realm
**Order:** Willshaper (Third Ideal)
**Spren:** Cadenza
**Strength:** 6, un-infused — the highest raw Strength score in the party, and a fact Kael brought up unprompted with some regularity
**Family:** Father [[Elias]] (Envisager cult leader; once a hardworking miner), mother (rescued from Envisager captivity), brother [[Jaro]] (cult plant, later gifted him a sword on his way out of the party)

## Overview
Somewhere between a traveling circus and a war camp full of grifters, a man learned that the fastest way to end an argument was to open a hole in the ground underneath it. That man was Kael — a former circus acrobat turned Willshaper, the party's designated heaviest hitter, and, against most odds, its moral center too, which says considerably more about the rest of the party than it does about him. He spent the whole campaign hauling around an unresolved relationship with a father who ran a Radiant-hunting death cult and an axehound he loved enough to commit ongoing, low-grade property crimes to keep. He did not survive the campaign. Almost none of the people who loved him got a clean goodbye — except, in the strange mercy of how it ended, him.

Officially: a Cohesion specialist capable of reshaping solid stone with a thought — fortifications, lock-picks, statues, and, most beloved of all, sinkholes, on command. Unofficially: the man who single-handedly excavated and built the party's entire secret underground lair beneath [[The Glyph Pool (Shop)|The Glyph Pool]] boutique, in total secrecy, purely with his bare hands and a stone-shaped Shard Ring — and somehow still found time to complain that nobody appreciated his cooking.

## Origin
Before any of this, Kael was a circus performer — an acrobat raised, if that's the word for it, under a ringmaster named Tan, whose treatment of him was bad enough that even years later, seeing his own abuser caged in front of him wasn't satisfying so much as it was a test. He carried the circus with him for the rest of his life, quite literally: he scaled masts and rigging with what the table simply logged as "circus energy," and it kept him and everyone around him alive more than once.

He also carried something else out of the circus without realizing it. Cadenza — a patient ball of light — had been quietly following him since his days under the big top, waiting for him to be ready. She finally revealed herself mid-campaign, in a hushed moment after a brutal whitespine fight, comforting a shaken Kael with an economy of vocabulary ("is okay") that somehow landed harder than a speech would have. It was the connection that let a boy who grew up performing tricks for an abusive ringmaster start to understand what the old stories about the Knights Radiant, half-listened to as a child, had actually been about.

His real Ideal-swearing debut, fittingly, wasn't about himself at all. Cornered by the cultist Shai, who grabbed his dog Handsy and threatened to kill him to bait Kael into a one-on-one duel, Kael didn't hesitate. He swore the First Ideal on the spot — "Strength before weakness. Life before death. Journey before destination." — then trapped her feet in stone with Cohesion and went to work. It set the pattern for the rest of his life: threaten something Kael loves, and he will simply become more dangerous.

## ★ The Trolley Problem: A Case Study in Elias's Cruelty
If you want to understand exactly what kind of father Elias was, this is the scene that answers it completely.

Kael tracked the Envisagers to their underground ritual site with Jaro and Handsy in tow, sensing pain and death soaked into the stones before he'd even seen a person. Then his father walked out of the dark — alive, when Kael had believed him dead, and radiantly overjoyed to see him, as though nothing about any of this required an explanation. It got worse fast: Jaro, the strange Iri traveling companion who'd tagged along since the Envisager dungeon, admitted on the spot that he was a cult member who had been tailing Kael the entire trip. Overwhelmed by the betrayal and the sheer wrongness radiating off the site, Kael swore the Second Ideal of the Willshapers — "I will seek freedom for those in bondage" — and exploded with Light.

Elias, entirely unbothered, sprang the real trap. He'd found and caged Tan, the ringmaster who'd spent Kael's childhood abusing him. He'd also found and bound Kael's mother. Then he offered his son a choice: honor the oath he'd just sworn and free his abuser, or save his own mother — but, Elias made clear, not both. The session ended there, mid-cliffhanger, and the actual moment of choice was lost to a corrupted recording before anyone could capture it for posterity. What survived is the aftermath: within one session, Kael, his mother, and Jaro were resting together at Leif's inn, which means that whatever happened in that ritual chamber, his mother walked out of it free. What it cost him, and what ultimately became of Tan, is a piece of Kael's history this wiki cannot recover — the tape simply isn't there. Some things not even a Willshaper can sinkhole his way back into.

## Oaths Sworn
1. First Ideal — sworn early in the campaign during the first fights against the Envisagers.
2. Second Ideal (Willshaper) — *"I will seek freedom for those in bondage,"* sworn confronting his father [[Elias]] during "the Trolley Problem" (choosing between freeing Tan or saving his mother).
3. Third Ideal — *"I will be the master of my own chains, that my strength may serve the world instead of those who would bind it,"* sworn while bound and drained by [[Sebarial]] aboard his ship.

## Key Moments

### Circus Ghosts & Quiet Miracles
- Cadenza, a patient ball of light, had been silently trailing Kael since his circus days and only revealed herself after a brutal whitespine fight, comforting him with a simple "is okay" that landed harder than any speech could have.
- Swore the First Ideal defending Handsy from a cultist's blade, immediately followed by trapping her feet in stone and going to work — the first of many times "hurt something Kael loves" turned out to be a fatal miscalculation.
- Climbed the mast of a barely-crewed, freshly-stolen brigantine "with circus energy" during the crew's first disastrous night at sea, then cooked a genuinely restorative meal for everyone that same evening. Turns out ringmasters teach you more than trauma.
- Nominated by his own teammate as secretly having "hidden cooking lore" during a party-wide debate over the worst chef in the group — right after being thrown under the bus first, on the logic that the strongest fighter in the party definitely couldn't cook.

### Stone Shaping, Sinkholes, and Other Minor War Crimes
- Built the party's entire secret underground lair beneath [[The Glyph Pool (Shop)|The Glyph Pool]] boutique by hand with Cohesion, while separately maintaining a lighteyed cover identity via a stone-shaped Shard Ring for roughly three hours at a time.
- Sinkholed the floor beneath a Conclave desk clerk mid-heist to create a distraction while Nucko lifted her keys — a plan whose contingency plan was "quietly destabilize an innocent civil servant's office."
- Elevated a chull, a wagon, and a stolen meteorite fifteen feet into the air on an instant stone rampart during a battlefield ambush, only to watch an enemy Ardent melt it into a ramp and send the meteorite rolling right back down. Not every plan survives contact with fabrial-powered spite.
- Free-climbed a sheer cliff face by carving handholds into it with Cohesion faster than Jaro could scale it the normal way, mid-rescue, purely to win a race neither of them needed to be having while a skyeel matriarch was actively hunting the party.
- Finished off that same skyeel matriarch by launching a stone spear at her from the ground the moment she flew off screeching for reinforcements — the kind of shot that ends arguments before they start.
- During downtime at the hideout, sinkholed Nucko in his own lair, slapped a stone mask over his face, tunneled beneath him, and lifted 10 marks off him to win a private bet — then sheepishly handed back double as an apology. The ethics were, at best, situational.
- Carved a hasty miniature stone statue of a heartbroken clerical worker under a tavern table to console her, moments after her actual date walked out on her — "massive creep-factor threat," by the table's own assessment, and it worked anyway.

### The Church of the Yips Saga
- Investigated the main Church of the Yips chapel with [[Torin]] and [[Gavlin]] and discovered his own stolen pet axehound, Hansy, enshrined in stained glass as "The Goodest Boy," first among the Yip-spren — and passed a Natural 20 Discipline check purely to keep himself from flipping the pulpit over on the spot.
- While Kael distracted the chapel's guardian, Shifty, with a devout-doubter routine and a hand-carved statue of a minor Yip that moved the man to tears, Torin quietly doused the office in Horn Eater White and burned the entire chapel down behind their backs.
- Weeks later, unwilling to let the chapel's one surviving stained-glass window stand as a "miracle" for the cult to point to, Kael launched a stone pillar at it from a hundred feet away — got caught mid-throw by Shifty, and somehow talked his way out of it with a Double Natural 20 Deception check, convincing him the shattered glass was a sign of divine providence.
- At a second, separate Church of the Yips chapel located in Highprince Aladar's camp, stone-shaped a locked door open, helped set the whole building ablaze from the inside alongside [[Nucko]] and Torin, then tunneled the entire party out beneath the foundation and resealed the exit behind them — while Torin put a bomb arrow clean through the last stained-glass window from six hundred yards out, purely for the theater of it.

### Party Life, Rivalries, and Regrettable Romance
- Recognized Luna — a giant Horn Eater he'd believed dead for months — mid-gambling-ring, poached him from the Kholin army on the spot with a stack of marks, then wrestled him for fun and pinned him with a Natural 20, cementing Luna as the crew's new hired muscle.
- Broke Varden's sternum with a single palm strike after Gavlin's own brother leveled a spear at him, then knelt over him and passed a 25 Intimidation check promising to finish the job if he ever disrespected Gavlin again. Gavlin healed him afterward, somewhat begrudgingly.
- Rebounded with a heartbroken tavern clerk named Liss the moment Torin's date with her collapsed in public — only to lose her right back to Torin days later over a bouquet and a pair of handmade shoes. Took the loss with more grace than the situation strictly required.
- Doubled down on the same losing cremling for three straight races out of sheer loyalty, lost every time, and was "vindicated" when it finally won the fourth — gambling as a matter of personal honor rather than probability.
- Confidently asserted he could beat everyone at the table in an arm wrestle, and the table agreed he probably could — his un-infused Strength of 6 was the highest in the party, a fact that came up constantly and always unprompted.
- Got formally enrolled in the bottom rank of the official Alethi dueling circuit and accepted a sparring invitation from an arrogant duelist who mocked him as "scrawny," using nothing but an 18 Intimidation check and a set of temporarily glowing blue eyes courtesy of a stolen Shard Ring.

### Sebarial, Chains, and the Third Ideal
- Went belowdecks alone on Sebarial's ship to check on captured prisoners, broke a stone-shaped key trying to pick a locked door, found one prisoner already vanished — and turned around to find Sebarial standing right behind him.
- Was grabbed by the throat, drained to zero Investiture, and stabbed for 19 damage by Sebarial, who then upgraded his ransom demand from one favor to two while Kael was still bleeding on the floor.
- Swore the Third Ideal in that exact moment of helplessness — bound, drained, and refusing on principle to accept servitude from anyone — and summoned a Shardblade that Sebarial's living cloth immediately forced back out of his hand. Newly minted Third Ideal Radiant, still completely powerless. Cadenza was thrilled anyway.
- Talked Sebarial down from servitude to "friendship" through a tense Leadership check, watched the demigod shake his hand and flip back to jovial small talk as if he hadn't just tortured him thirty seconds prior, and had to simply live with that.

### The Reckonings
- Was personally singled out by Nale — an ancient, unsettling Azish Vizier — who told Kael to his face that he'd kill him for his past wrongdoings if he ever had the legal jurisdiction to do it. Kael looked visibly shaken. Understandably.
- Demanded and received a binding Blood Oath from [[Nucko]] after the Ghostblood reveal — unsheathed his own blade, sliced his palm open, and made clear that any future deception between them would end in a duel to the death. The oath never got broken. There simply wasn't time left for it to be tested.

### The Final Battle
- Confirmed that Sebarial's recovered axehound, Hansy, really was Hansy using a set of secret verification commands, moments before Sebarial revealed his true plan: harvest Voidlight from the incoming Everstorm, key his soul to Odium, and personally kill a god.
- Fought through a battle where enemy fabrials turned his own clothing rigid, ripped himself free of a grapple on a Natural 20, used Regrowth to heal himself mid-combat, and still landed a 22 with a disadvantaged Shardblade swing that cut an enemy minion clean in half.
- Was taken hostage by Sebarial mid-fight and used as leverage, absorbing severe injuries while the rest of the party scrambled to free him and finish Sebarial off for good with the stolen Raysium dagger.
- Succumbed to his wounds on the outpost floor despite everyone's efforts to save him — the campaign's second PC death, after Theren, and one of the two losses that would eventually cost the party [[Nucko]] as well.
- In his final moment, crossed into the Cognitive Realm and was met not by the broken cult leader who'd tormented him for a session and a half, but by the memory of his father as he'd been before any of it — a warm, hardworking miner who'd once just taught his son how to smile. Kael took his hand and stepped into the Beyond.
- Was honored afterward with a full party memorial: Gavlin remembered his unconditional loyalty, Ravan draped his own finest clothing over Kael's chest to mark him a true champion, and Nucko, gently and for the last time, transformed his body into a permanent stone monument.

## Relationships
- [[Nucko]] — blood-oath bound after the Ghostblood reveal; the oath was never broken, and never got the chance to be tested. Kael's death, alongside Theren's, was a direct cause of Nucko's eventual departure from the party.
- [[Jaro]] — brother, betrayer, and cult plant who spent an entire cross-country trip spying on his own sibling. Reconciled, at least partially, before Jaro left the party himself — wept, and gifted Kael a master duelist's sword on his way out.
- [[Elias]] — father, Envisager cult leader, and architect of the Trolley Problem. Appeared to Kael one final time in the Cognitive Realm — not as the man who built a cult around his own son, but as the miner he'd been before any of that happened.
- [[Luna]] — sparring rival turned hired muscle, recruited on sight the moment Kael recognized him at a gambling ring.
- [[Torin]] — chapel-arson accomplice (twice over), occasional romantic rival over Liss, and one of the last people fighting beside Kael at the Shattered Plains outpost.
- [[Gavlin]] — fellow party member, whose family honor Kael defended by breaking his brother's sternum, and who eulogized Kael's "unconditional loyalty" at his memorial.
- [[Sebarial]] — captor, torturer, and eventually killer. Forced Kael's Third Ideal out of him at knifepoint, then took his life in the final battle against him — a death [[Nucko]] would go on to personally avenge.
- Liss — a Kholin clerical worker Kael briefly rebounded with after a Torin/Liss blowup; lost her right back to Torin within the same string of sessions.
